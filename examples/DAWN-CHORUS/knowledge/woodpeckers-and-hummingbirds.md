# DAWN-CHORUS — Woodpeckers and Hummingbirds of Salmonberry Creek

Species accounts for the woodpeckers and hummingbirds at Salmonberry Creek Preserve, with notes on drumming and hummingbird feeders. Drumming patterns and calls are also described in songs-and-calls.md; Downy and Hairy Woodpeckers are compared in look-alikes.md.

---

## Woodpeckers

### Pileated Woodpecker

The largest woodpecker in the region, nearly crow-sized: black with bold white face stripes and a flaming red crest; males also have a red moustache stripe. It chops large rectangular holes in dead and dying trees to reach carpenter ants, leaving piles of wood chips at the base. Listen for its wild "wuk-wuk-wuk" call and slow, powerful drumming in the Cedar Forest. Its old nest holes become homes for owls, ducks and bats. Present year-round.

### Northern Flicker

A large brown woodpecker with a black-barred back, a black crescent on the chest and a spotted belly. Western ("red-shafted") flickers flash salmon-red under their wings and tail in flight, and show a white rump patch. Flickers often feed on the ground, eating ants. Calls a loud "kleer!" and a long "wick-wick-wick". Present year-round, especially around the Sedge Meadow.

### Downy Woodpecker

The smallest woodpecker here, sparrow-sized: black and white with a white back stripe and a short, stubby bill; males have a small red patch on the back of the head. Often feeds on alder branches and cattail stems. Calls a soft "pik" and a descending whinny. Present year-round.

### Hairy Woodpecker

Looks like a larger Downy Woodpecker with a much longer, chisel-like bill, nearly as long as its head. Prefers larger trees in the Cedar Forest. Its call is a sharp, loud "peek!". Present year-round.

### Red-breasted Sapsucker

A medium woodpecker with an entirely red head and breast, a black back with white bars and a white wing stripe. Sapsuckers drill neat horizontal rows of small holes (sap wells) in alders, birches and hemlocks, then return to lick the sap and eat the insects it attracts. Rufous Hummingbirds feed at sapsucker wells in early spring, before many flowers bloom. Its drumming is irregular, like Morse code. Present year-round.

### Why Woodpeckers Drum

Drumming is how woodpeckers sing: they hammer rapidly on a resonant surface to claim territory and attract mates, mostly from January to May. Each species has its own rhythm. Woodpeckers sometimes drum on metal gutters, chimney caps and signs because the sound carries farther; covering the spot with a cloth for a few weeks usually ends it. How woodpeckers avoid concussions is still being studied; their small brain size seems to be a big part of it.

## Hummingbirds

### Anna's Hummingbird

A medium-sized green hummingbird. Males have an iridescent rose-pink throat and crown that flashes brilliantly in the sun and looks dark in shade; females have a small patch of pink on the throat. Anna's Hummingbirds live here year-round, surviving freezing winter nights by dropping into torpor, a deep energy-saving sleep. Once found only in California, they spread north through the Pacific Northwest from the 1960s, helped by garden flowers and feeders. Males perform dramatic courtship dives from December to June.

### Rufous Hummingbird

A small, feisty hummingbird. Males are bright orange-rufous with a glowing orange-red throat; females are green with rufous sides. The first Rufous Hummingbirds arrive in late February and March, timed to the blooming of red-flowering currant and salmonberry, and aggressively chase other hummingbirds from flowers and feeders. They make one of the longest migrations for their body size of any bird, from Mexico to as far as Alaska. Most leave by August.

### Hummingbird Feeders

Mix 1 part white table sugar with 4 parts water; boiling is not needed if you use clean water and change it often. Never use honey, brown sugar, artificial sweeteners or red dye. Change the nectar every 2 to 3 days, daily in hot weather, and clean the feeder each time to prevent mold. In freezing weather, Anna's Hummingbirds depend on feeders: bring the feeder in at night and put it out at first light, or keep it just above freezing with a clip-on light or hand warmer.

### Hummingbird Flowers

Native plants that feed hummingbirds at the preserve include red-flowering currant (February to April), salmonberry (March to May), orange honeysuckle (May to July) and red columbine (April to June). In winter, Anna's Hummingbirds eat tiny insects and spiders, visit sapsucker wells and depend heavily on feeders.
