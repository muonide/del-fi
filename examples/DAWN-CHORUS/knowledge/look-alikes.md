# DAWN-CHORUS — Look-Alikes: Telling Similar Birds Apart

Pairs of birds at Salmonberry Creek Preserve that are often confused, with the one or two field marks that settle it. Full species accounts are in forest-songbirds.md, marsh-and-water-birds.md, owls-and-raptors.md and woodpeckers-and-hummingbirds.md.

---

## Woodpeckers and Hawks

### Downy Woodpecker vs Hairy Woodpecker

Look at the bill. A Downy's bill is short and stubby, much shorter than its head is deep; a Hairy's bill is long and chisel-like, nearly as long as its head. Hairy is also bigger (robin-sized versus sparrow-sized) and gives a sharp "peek!" where the Downy gives a soft "pik". Downy often has black spots on its white outer tail feathers; Hairy's are clean white.

### Cooper's Hawk vs Sharp-shinned Hawk

The hardest pair in birding, and sizes overlap because females are larger than males. In flight, a Cooper's head sticks out well past the wrists, while a Sharp-shinned's small head barely shows. The Cooper's tail is rounded at the tip with a broad white band; the Sharp-shinned's tail is square or notched. Sharp-shinned flaps quickly and stiffly; Cooper's flaps more slowly and deeply. When unsure, "accipiter" (either one) is a fine answer.

### Bald Eagle vs Turkey Vulture, soaring

Both are big and dark overhead. A Turkey Vulture holds its wings raised in a shallow V and rocks unsteadily, with pale flight feathers contrasting with a black front half of the wing and a tiny head. A Bald Eagle soars on flat, plank-like wings, steady as a table, with a big head and bill sticking out; young eagles are blotched with white underneath.

## Thrushes and Chickadees

### Swainson's Thrush vs Hermit Thrush

Check the tail and the season. A Hermit Thrush has a reddish tail that contrasts with its browner back, and slowly raises and lowers it; a Swainson's Thrush is evenly coloured and has a bold buffy eye-ring. Hermit Thrushes are here from October to April, Swainson's from May to September, so in winter a spotted thrush is almost always a Hermit. Their songs differ too: see songs-and-calls.md.

### Varied Thrush vs American Robin

Both are orange-breasted thrushes of the same size. A Varied Thrush has an orange eyebrow, orange wing bars and a black band across the breast; a robin has a plain grey back, a white eye-ring and no breast band. Varied Thrushes stay in the forest; robins prefer lawns and meadows.

### Black-capped Chickadee vs Chestnut-backed Chickadee

The back and flanks: rich chestnut on a Chestnut-backed, grey back and buffy flanks on a Black-capped. The Chestnut-backed also has a brown cap rather than a black one. Black-capped prefer alders and deciduous trees; Chestnut-backed prefer conifers.

### Golden-crowned Kinglet vs Ruby-crowned Kinglet

A Golden-crowned has a bold black-and-white striped face and a yellow crown stripe. A Ruby-crowned has a plain face with a broken white eye-ring, its red crown usually hidden, and constantly flicks its wings.

## Sparrows, Finches and Swallows

### Song Sparrow vs Fox Sparrow

Both are dark and heavily marked here. A Song Sparrow is streaky with a central breast spot and a long tail it pumps in flight. A Fox Sparrow, a winter visitor from October to April, is chunkier and darker, with a thick triangular bill and heavy spots that merge into a dark blotch on the chest; it scratches in the leaf litter with both feet like a towhee.

### House Finch vs Purple Finch

Look at the males' colour and the females' face. A male House Finch is red only on the head, breast and rump, with brown streaks on its sides and belly; a male Purple Finch looks dipped in raspberry juice, with colour washing the back and wings and few streaks below. Female Purple Finches have a bold white eyebrow and dark cheek; female House Finches have a plain face. Purple Finches favour the forest; House Finches favour towns and feeders.

### Tree Swallow vs Violet-green Swallow

Both are dark above and white below. The Violet-green's white wraps up around and above its eye and forms white patches on both sides of the rump, visible from above; a Tree Swallow's white stops below the eye, and its back is a steelier blue-green.

## Owls, Jays and Crows

### Western Screech-Owl vs Northern Saw-whet Owl

A screech-owl is grey with ear tufts and yellow eyes; a saw-whet is brown with a round head, no ear tufts and a white-streaked face. Their calls differ completely: a bouncing ball of hoots versus a steady, truck-backing-up tooting.

### Barred Owl vs Spotted Owl

A big brown owl with dark eyes at the preserve is a Barred Owl. The Northern Spotted Owl, which has white spots rather than bars, lives only in old-growth forests and is not found at the preserve; any report of one here would need a photo.

### Steller's Jay vs California Scrub-Jay

A Steller's Jay has a black head and a tall black crest. A California Scrub-Jay, a newer arrival in the Puget lowlands, has no crest, a blue head and wings, a grey-brown back and a white throat with a blue necklace. Scrub-jays prefer open neighbourhoods and oaks; Steller's Jays prefer conifers.

### American Crow vs Common Raven

A raven is much bigger, with a heavy bill, shaggy throat and wedge-shaped tail in flight, and it croaks "gronk". A crow has a smaller bill, a fan-shaped tail and a "caw". Ravens often soar and tumble in pairs; crows flap steadily and gather in noisy groups.
