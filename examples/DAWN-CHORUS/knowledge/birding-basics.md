# DAWN-CHORUS — Birding Basics and Using This Node

How to identify birds, how to watch them without disturbing them, how to contribute to bird science, and how to get the most out of the DAWN-CHORUS node at Salmonberry Creek Preserve.

---

## Identifying Birds

### How to Identify a Bird

Work through four clues, roughly in this order: size and shape (compare to a sparrow, robin or crow; note bill shape and tail length), behaviour (creeping up trunks, hovering, flicking wings, scratching the ground), habitat (forest, creek, marsh, meadow) and colour pattern (face stripes, wing bars, breast spots). Then add sound and season: a spotted thrush in January is a Hermit Thrush, in June a Swainson's. When asking this node, describe as many clues as you can in one message.

### Identifying Birds by Sound

Start with the common voices: American Robin, Song Sparrow, Spotted Towhee, Pacific Wren and Steller's Jay make up much of what you hear at the preserve. Learn one new song a week, and use mnemonics such as "who cooks for you" (Barred Owl) or "quick, three beers" (Olive-sided Flycatcher). Describe what you hear to the node: pitch (high, low), quality (whistled, buzzy, flute-like, harsh), pattern (repeated, trilled, rising, falling) and length. See songs-and-calls.md.

### Binoculars

Binoculars marked 8x42 are a good all-round choice: 8 times magnification, with 42 mm lenses that gather light in the dim forest. To find a bird, keep your eyes on it and raise the binoculars into your line of sight, rather than searching through them. The preserve stewards keep two loaner pairs at the trailhead kiosk on weekend mornings.

## Watching Birds Responsibly

### Birding Ethics

Keep your distance: if a bird changes its behaviour, stops feeding or gives alarm calls, you are too close. Never approach nests, the heron colony or roosting owls, and do not trim branches for a better view or photo. Stay on trails. Do not play recorded songs or calls from March 1 to August 31 at the preserve, and keep it brief the rest of the year; playback pulls birds away from feeding and nesting. Never feed wildlife. Share rare nesting birds' locations only with the stewards.

### Photographing Birds

Use a long lens instead of getting closer, never flush a bird to photograph it in flight, and do not use flash on owls at night. Do not bait owls with mice. If you find a roosting owl, enjoy it quietly and leave; a crowd of visitors can drive it away.

## Contributing to Bird Science

### eBird

eBird, run by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology, is a free app and website where birders record what they see and hear. Your checklists become data used by scientists and conservationists around the world. The preserve has its own eBird hotspot, "Salmonberry Creek Preserve". The free Merlin Bird ID app, also from the Cornell Lab, identifies birds from photos and live sound on your phone.

### Community Bird Counts

The Great Backyard Bird Count takes place over four days in mid-February, and anyone can count birds for 15 minutes or more. The Christmas Bird Count runs between December 14 and January 5; the preserve is inside a count circle, and volunteers of every skill level are welcome. Ask the stewards to join.

## Using This Node

### Commands

Send DAWN-CHORUS a direct message from any Meshtastic app. Ask questions in plain words, such as "what sounds like a flute spiralling upward?". Useful commands: !data shows the listening station's live detections; !board shows recent sightings posted by visitors; !post followed by a message adds your own sighting; !topics lists what the node knows about; !more sends the next part of a long answer. The node answers one question per sender every 30 seconds.

### Asking About Live Detections

The node answers some questions straight from the listening station without using its language model, so they are fast and exact. Ask "what's singing right now?", "any owls tonight?", "how was the dawn chorus this morning?", "what birds were detected today?" or "any new arrivals?", or send !data for everything at once. See birdnet-station.md for how the station works and how far to trust it.

### Posting Sightings

Share what you see with !post, for example "!post Pileated Woodpecker at Cedar Trail footbridge 2, 8:15am". Include the species, where and when. Posts appear on !board for 24 hours and the stewards copy notable ones into the sighting log. Do not post the locations of active nests or roosting owls.
