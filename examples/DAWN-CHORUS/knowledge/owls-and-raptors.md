# DAWN-CHORUS — Owls and Raptors of Salmonberry Creek

Species accounts for the owls, hawks, eagles and falcons at Salmonberry Creek Preserve. Owls are mostly heard at night; their calls are in songs-and-calls.md. For telling similar hawks apart, see look-alikes.md. Please never use recorded owl calls on the preserve: it stresses nesting owls and the small birds they eat.

---

## Owls

### Barred Owl

A large, round-headed owl with no ear tufts, dark brown eyes, and brown-and-white barring on the chest and streaks on the belly. The most commonly heard owl on the preserve, calling "who-cooks-for-you, who-cooks-for-you-all" year-round, and sometimes active by day in the Cedar Forest. Barred Owls spread into the Northwest from the east during the 1900s and now compete with the threatened Northern Spotted Owl in old-growth forests.

### Great Horned Owl

A big, powerful owl with long ear tufts, yellow eyes and a white throat patch. It hunts anything from mice to skunks and other owls. Great Horned Owls nest earlier than almost any other bird here, laying eggs in old hawk or crow nests in January and February. Heard around the Sedge Meadow edge.

### Western Screech-Owl

A small grey owl, about 8 inches tall, with ear tufts and yellow eyes, perfectly camouflaged against bark. It nests in tree cavities and old flicker holes near the creek and calls its "bouncing ball" series of hoots at night year-round. Numbers have fallen in parts of the Puget lowlands, partly because Barred Owls eat them.

### Northern Saw-whet Owl

A tiny owl, about the size of a robin, with a big round head, no ear tufts and a white-streaked brown face. Its tooting call, like a truck backing up, is heard mostly from February to April. By day saw-whets roost in dense young conifers, often at eye level, and are very hard to spot.

### Barn Owl

A pale, long-legged owl with a white, heart-shaped face and dark eyes. Instead of hooting it gives a long, harsh screech or hiss. Barn Owls hunt voles over the Sedge Meadow at night and can find prey in total darkness by hearing alone. Present year-round but seldom seen.

## Hawks, Eagles and Vultures

### Bald Eagle

A huge raptor with a 7-foot wingspan. Adults have a white head and tail and a yellow bill, a look they reach at about five years old; younger birds are mottled brown and white. Bald Eagles perch on the tallest snags around the Beaver Pond and chase Ospreys to steal their fish. Their call is a weak, high chittering, not a scream. Present year-round.

### Osprey

A large, long-winged fish hawk, brown above and white below, with a dark stripe through the eye. In flight its wings bend at the wrist into a shallow M. It hovers over the water, then plunges feet-first to catch fish. A pair nests on the tallest snag at the Snag Overlook from April to August; they winter in Mexico and Central America.

### Red-tailed Hawk

The common big hawk of the region: a stocky soaring hawk, usually with a brick-red tail as an adult, a pale chest and a dark belly band. It perches on snags and poles around the Sedge Meadow watching for voles. Its hoarse scream, "keee-arr", is the hawk call used in films. Present year-round.

### Cooper's Hawk

A crow-sized, long-tailed hawk built for speed through trees, with a slate-grey back and fine rusty barring below. It ambushes songbirds, including at the trailhead feeders. Present year-round. See look-alikes.md for telling it from the smaller Sharp-shinned Hawk.

### Sharp-shinned Hawk

A small, fast hawk, jay-sized in males, with short rounded wings and a long square-tipped tail. It hunts small songbirds in fast, twisting chases through the forest. Most often seen from September to April, when northern birds pass through and spend the winter.

### Northern Harrier

A slim, long-winged, long-tailed hawk with a white rump patch that flies low over the Sedge Meadow in a tilting glide, hunting voles by sound as well as sight. Its owl-like facial disc helps it hear. Males are pale grey ("grey ghosts"); females and young are brown. Winter visitor, October to April.

### Turkey Vulture

A large, dark bird with a small, bare red head (grey on young birds). It soars with its wings raised in a shallow V and rocks from side to side, rarely flapping. Vultures find carrion by smell, which few birds can do. Present March to October; they migrate south for the winter.

## Falcons

### Merlin

A small, powerful dark falcon, a bit bigger than a jay, that hunts songbirds and dragonflies in fast, direct flight over the Sedge Meadow and Beaver Pond. It perches on the tops of snags and calls a rapid "ki-ki-ki-ki". Most common from September to April.

### American Kestrel

North America's smallest falcon, about the size of a robin. Males have blue-grey wings and a rusty back and tail; both sexes have two black "moustache" stripes on the face. Kestrels hover over the meadow looking for grasshoppers and voles. Uncommon at the preserve, mostly in spring and autumn migration.
