# DAWN-CHORUS — Salmonberry Creek Preserve Guide

Salmonberry Creek Preserve is a 160-acre wetland and forest preserve in the Puget Sound lowlands of western Washington. This guide covers the habitats, trails, best birding spots, rules and facilities. For what lives here, see forest-songbirds.md, marsh-and-water-birds.md, owls-and-raptors.md and woodpeckers-and-hummingbirds.md.

---

## Habitats

### Cedar Forest

About half the preserve is second-growth forest of western redcedar, Douglas-fir and western hemlock, 80 to 100 years old, with sword fern and moss underneath. Dark, damp and quiet: listen for Pacific Wren, Varied Thrush, Brown Creeper, Golden-crowned Kinglet and Chestnut-backed Chickadee. Barred Owls roost here by day.

### Creek Corridor

Salmonberry Creek runs the length of the preserve, lined with red alder, bigleaf maple and dense salmonberry thickets. This is the best place for Swainson's Thrush, Wilson's Warbler, Western Flycatcher and Spotted Towhee in spring and summer.

### Beaver Pond and Cattail Marsh

A beaver dam built in 2019 flooded 12 acres of old pasture, creating open water ringed with cattails and dead standing trees (snags). Look for Wood Duck, Hooded Merganser, Great Blue Heron, Belted Kingfisher, Red-winged Blackbird, Marsh Wren, Virginia Rail and swallows.

### Sedge Meadow

A wet meadow on the east side, mowed once a year in September. Good for swallows hawking insects, Savannah and Song Sparrows, hunting raptors (Northern Harrier in winter, Red-tailed Hawk year-round) and Northern Flickers feeding on ants.

## Trails

### Creek Loop

1.2 miles, flat gravel, 30–45 minutes. Follows Salmonberry Creek through alder and salmonberry. Best trail for the dawn chorus in May and June. Leashed dogs allowed.

### Cedar Trail

0.8 miles through the Cedar Forest, with roots and two footbridges; can be muddy October to May. Quietest trail on the preserve and the best for owls at dusk. No dogs.

### Marsh Boardwalk

0.4 miles of wheelchair-accessible boardwalk from the trailhead to the Beaver Pond Blind. No dogs. Open dawn to dusk like the rest of the preserve.

### Meadow Path

0.6 miles of mown grass along the Sedge Meadow edge, connecting the Creek Loop to the Snag Overlook. Leashed dogs allowed.

### Snag Overlook

A 200-yard spur off the Meadow Path to a bench facing a stand of tall dead Douglas-firs. Ospreys nest on the tallest snag from April to August, and the spur closes March 15 to July 31 to protect them. Olive-sided Flycatchers sing from the snag tops in late May and June.

## Best Spots

### Beaver Pond Blind

A roofed wooden blind at the end of the Marsh Boardwalk with viewing slots at sitting and standing height. The best single spot on the preserve: ducks, herons, kingfishers and swallows, and from February to July the heron colony in the cottonwoods across the pond (view it only from the blind). The listening station's microphone hangs under the blind's eaves; see birdnet-station.md.

### Heron Colony

The best place to see nesting herons. About 20 Great Blue Heron nests in the black cottonwoods on the far side of the Beaver Pond. Birds return in February, and chicks are loud and visible in May and June. Never approach the colony: disturbed herons can abandon a whole season's nests.

### Alder Bend

The sharp bend in the Creek Loop about 0.5 miles from the trailhead, where the creek slows and salmonberry is thickest. In May it is the most reliable place to hear Swainson's Thrush, and in winter mixed flocks of chickadees, kinglets and Bushtits pass through.

## Rules and Facilities

### Preserve Rules

Open dawn to dusk year-round. Stay on trails and boardwalks. Do not play recorded bird songs or calls (playback) anywhere on the preserve from March 1 to August 31, the nesting season, and keep it brief the rest of the year. No drones. Do not feed wildlife. Dogs must be leashed and are allowed only on the Creek Loop and Meadow Path.

### Dogs

Can I bring my dog? Yes, on a leash, on the Creek Loop and Meadow Path. Dogs are not allowed on the Cedar Trail or the Marsh Boardwalk, to protect ground-nesting birds, the marsh and the heron colony. Please pick up after your dog.

### Trailhead and Facilities

The trailhead parking lot (24 cars) is at the end of Salmonberry Road, with a pit toilet, an information kiosk and a trail map. There is no water on the preserve. The Del-Fi node, DAWN-CHORUS, shares a weatherproof box in the roof of the Beaver Pond Blind with the listening station; send it a direct message from any Meshtastic app anywhere on the preserve.

### Reporting Problems

Report downed trees, flooding or vandalism by posting on the node's board (!post) and emailing the volunteer stewards at stewards@salmonberry-preserve.example. For injured wildlife, see found-a-bird.md.
