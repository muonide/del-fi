# DAWN-CHORUS — Marsh and Water Birds of Salmonberry Creek

Species accounts for the birds of the Beaver Pond, the cattail marsh and the creek at Salmonberry Creek Preserve. The Beaver Pond Blind at the end of the Marsh Boardwalk is the best place to see most of them; see preserve-guide.md. Their calls are described in songs-and-calls.md.

---

## Herons and Kingfishers

### Great Blue Heron

A tall grey-blue heron, about 4 feet tall with a 6-foot wingspan, that stands motionless in the shallows and strikes fish, frogs and voles with its dagger bill. In flight it folds its neck into an S and trails its long legs. About 20 pairs nest in the heron colony in the cottonwoods across the Beaver Pond from February to July. Present year-round.

### Green Heron

A small, compact heron with a dark green back, chestnut neck and yellow-orange legs, often hunched on a low branch over the water. Green Herons sometimes drop bait, such as a feather or insect, on the water to lure fish. Uncommon at the Beaver Pond from May to September.

### Belted Kingfisher

A stocky, big-headed blue-grey bird with a shaggy crest and a heavy bill. It hovers over the water and dives headfirst for small fish, and announces itself with a loud rattle. Unusually, the female is more colourful than the male: she has a rusty band across her belly. Present year-round at the Beaver Pond and creek.

## Ducks, Geese and Grebes

### Wood Duck

The male is one of the most ornate ducks in the world: green crested head with white stripes, red eye, chestnut breast. Wood Ducks nest in tree cavities and the nest boxes on the Beaver Pond snags. A day after hatching, the ducklings jump from the nest hole, sometimes 30 feet up, and are led to the water. Females give a squealing "oo-eek!" when flushed. Present year-round.

### Hooded Merganser

A small diving duck with a thin, serrated bill for catching fish. The male raises a striking white, black-bordered fan crest; the female has a shaggy cinnamon crest. Most common on the Beaver Pond from October to April; a few pairs nest in the pond's nest boxes.

### Bufflehead

A small, bouncy diving duck. Males are bright white with a black back and a big dark head with a white patch; females are grey-brown with a white cheek spot. A winter visitor from October to April, diving constantly in the open water of the Beaver Pond.

### Ring-necked Duck

A diving duck of small ponds. The male has a peaked black head, grey sides and a white ring near the tip of the bill; the ring on his neck is almost impossible to see. Winter visitor, October to April.

### Mallard

The familiar green-headed dabbling duck, present year-round. Mallards feed by tipping up rather than diving. The female's loud descending "quack-quack-quack" is the classic duck sound; males make a quieter, raspy call.

### Canada Goose

A large brown goose with a black neck and white chinstrap. Pairs nest on the beaver lodge and muskrat mounds in April and May, and parents with goslings defend the boardwalk fiercely; give them room.

### Pied-billed Grebe

A small brown diving bird with a thick, chicken-like bill that has a black ring in summer. Instead of flying away from danger it can sink slowly until only its head shows. Its loud call, "cuck-cuck-cuck-cow-cow-cowp", echoes across the pond in spring. Present year-round.

## Marsh Songbirds

### Red-winged Blackbird

Males are glossy black with red shoulder patches edged in yellow, which they flare while singing "conk-la-ree!" from cattail tops. Females are streaky brown and look like large sparrows. Males return to the marsh in February and defend territories aggressively, even chasing hawks and herons. Present year-round in varying numbers.

### Marsh Wren

A small brown wren with a white eyebrow and a streaked back, clinging sideways to cattail stems with its tail cocked. Males build several dome-shaped "dummy" nests woven into the cattails, and females choose one to use. Sings day and night from April to August; some stay year-round.

### Common Yellowthroat

A small warbler that skulks low in the cattails and sedges. Males are olive and bright yellow with a black mask like a bandit; females lack the mask. Present May to September, singing "witchety-witchety-witchety".

## Rails

### Virginia Rail

A chicken-like marsh bird about the size of a robin but with a long, slightly curved red bill, rusty breast and grey cheeks. Rails are narrow-bodied ("thin as a rail") and slip between cattail stems. Heard far more than seen: listen for grunts and "kid-ick" calls at dawn from the Beaver Pond Blind. Present year-round.

### Sora

A small, plump rail with a short yellow bill, a black face and grey breast. Soras creep along the cattail edges at dawn and dusk and give a descending whinny. Present April to September.

## Swallows

### Tree Swallow

Iridescent blue-green above and bright white below. Tree Swallows nest in the nest boxes and old woodpecker holes in the Beaver Pond snags, and hawk insects low over the water and meadow. Among the first spring arrivals, in early March; gone by September.

### Violet-green Swallow

Green and violet above, with white below that wraps up over the eye and white patches on each side of the rump. Often flies higher than Tree Swallows, giving thin chirps. Present March to September.

### Barn Swallow

A deep-blue swallow with a rusty throat, buffy underparts and a long, deeply forked tail. Barn Swallows build mud-cup nests under the trailhead kiosk roof and the footbridges. Present April to September, wintering in South America.
